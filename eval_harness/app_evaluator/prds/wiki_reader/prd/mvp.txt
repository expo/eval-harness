# Product Requirements Document: Wiki Reader

## Overview

Wiki Reader is a mobile application that provides a clean, customizable interface for browsing Wikipedia. It replaces the default Wikipedia website or app experience with a more focused reading app that supports multi-language browsing, bookmarking, history tracking, and extensive display customization.

---

## Goals

- Provide a fast, distraction-reduced reading experience for Wikipedia articles
- Support discovery through random article browsing
- Allow users to save articles and track their reading history
- Offer extensive personalization of appearance and reading settings
- Support Wikipedia in all available languages

---

## Target Platform

Mobile (Android and iOS). The app should feel native to each platform while maintaining a consistent feature set.

---

## Navigation Structure

The app uses a side drawer navigation menu accessible from the home screen. The drawer provides top-level navigation to:

1. **Home** — main browsing screen
2. **Saved** — bookmarked articles
3. **History** — recently viewed articles
4. **Settings** — preferences and configuration
5. **About** — app information and credits

---

## Screens & Features

### 1. Home Screen

The home screen is the primary interface for reading Wikipedia articles.

#### Header
- A **menu button** that opens the side navigation drawer
- A **search bar** with placeholder text "Search Wikipedia" that allows users to search for articles by title
- A **shuffle button** that loads a random Wikipedia article

#### Article Display
- A full-screen web view renders the Wikipedia article
- On app launch, a random Wikipedia article is automatically loaded
- The article respects the user's selected Wikipedia language setting
- A **progress bar** appears at the top of the screen while an article is loading and disappears once fully loaded
- Users can **pull down to refresh** the current article

#### Floating Toolbar
A bottom floating toolbar appears over the article and contains the following actions:
- **Shuffle** — load a new random article
- **Translate** — translate the current article (placeholder for future feature)
- **Share** — share the article link (placeholder for future feature)
- **Download** — save the article for offline reading (placeholder for future feature)
- **Scroll to top** — instantly scroll back to the top of the article
- **Search** — open article search (placeholder for future feature)

When the **Immersive Mode** setting is enabled, this toolbar and the search bar hide while the user is scrolling down, and reappear when the user scrolls up or stops.

---

### 2. Article Page View

When opening an article from Saved or History, the article is shown in a dedicated page view:

- The article title is displayed in the page header
- A progress bar shows loading state
- The full Wikipedia article is rendered in a web view
- Display settings (theme, font size, font style, image settings, etc.) apply here as they do on the Home screen

---

### 3. Saved Articles Screen

Allows users to manage articles they have bookmarked.

#### Header
- Title: "Saved"
- A **delete button** in the header to trigger article deletion

#### Empty State
- An icon and the message "No saved articles"
- Supporting text: "Articles you save will appear here"

#### Article List (when articles exist)
- If saved articles span multiple Wikipedia languages, **language filter chips** appear at the top of the list, allowing the user to toggle which languages are shown
- Articles are grouped by language when multiple languages are present; groups are not shown if only one language exists
- Each article item displays:
  - A placeholder avatar showing the first letter of the article title
  - The article title
  - The article description or excerpt
- Tapping an article opens it in the Article Page View

#### Deletion
- Tapping the header delete button opens a dialog with context-sensitive options:
  - If an article is selected: option to delete that specific article
  - If no article is selected: option to delete all saved articles
- User must confirm before deletion proceeds

---

### 4. History Screen

Shows all Wikipedia articles the user has previously viewed, in reverse chronological order.

#### Header
- Title: "History"
- A **delete button** that clears all history immediately (no confirmation required)

#### Empty State
- An icon and the message "No history items"
- Supporting text: "Articles you view will appear here"

#### Article List (when history exists)
- Articles are grouped under date headers:
  - "Today"
  - "Yesterday"
  - Specific date for older entries (e.g., "June 5")
- Within each group, articles appear in reverse chronological order
- Each article item shows:
  - Article title
  - Article description
  - Time the article was viewed (e.g., "2:30 PM"), displayed on the right
- Tapping an article opens it in the Article Page View

---

### 5. Settings Screen

The Settings screen organizes all user preferences into collapsible sections.

---

#### Appearance

**Color Scheme**
- Opens a color picker dialog
- User selects from 13 preset accent colors:
  - Coral, Pink, Magenta, Blue, Cyan, Light Cyan, Teal, Green, Lime, Yellow, Orange, Peach, White
- The currently selected color is indicated with a checkmark
- The selected color is used as the app's primary accent color throughout the UI

**Theme**
- Opens a dialog with three options:
  - Light
  - Dark
  - System default (follows the device's current setting)
- Default: System default

**Black Theme** *(toggle)*
- When enabled and the app is in dark mode, uses pure black (#000000) instead of dark gray as the background
- Supporting text explains that this applies only in dark mode

---

#### Language

**Wikipedia Language**
- Opens a searchable bottom sheet listing all available Wikipedia language editions (100+)
- Each language is shown with its native name and its Wikipedia name in that language
- A "Recent languages" section shows previously selected languages at the top
- A search bar filters the list in real time
- Selecting a language changes the language of all Wikipedia articles loaded in the app

**App Language**
- Opens a searchable bottom sheet for the app's interface language
- Options include "System default" and all supported locales
- A search bar filters the list in real time
- Changing this setting translates all app UI strings into the selected language

---

#### Typography

**Font Style** *(toggle)*
- Switches article text between **Sans-serif** and **Serif** typeface
- Default: Sans-serif

**Font Size** *(slider)*
- Adjusts the base font size for article text
- Range: 10–22px
- Current value is shown numerically alongside the slider

---

#### Content & Display

**Data Saver** *(toggle)*
- When enabled, images are not loaded on the home feed
- Images in individual article page views are unaffected

**Feed** *(toggle)*
- When enabled, the home screen shows trending Wikipedia articles, news, and other discovery content on startup
- When disabled, the home screen opens directly to the last or a random article

**Expand Sections** *(toggle)*
- When enabled, all collapsible sections within an article are expanded by default
- When disabled, sections start collapsed and the user expands them manually

**Image Background** *(toggle)*
- When enabled, a white background is added behind transparent images
- Improves visibility of images that use transparency in dark mode

**Immersive Mode** *(toggle)*
- When enabled, the search bar and floating toolbar hide automatically while the user scrolls down, and reappear when scrolling stops or reverses
- Enabled by default on smaller screens
- Designed to maximize article reading area

**Render Math Expressions** *(toggle)*
- When enabled, LaTeX and other mathematical markup within articles is rendered as formatted math
- When disabled, raw markup may be shown, improving performance at the cost of readability
- Supporting text notes that enabling this uses additional data

---

#### History & Search

**Save History** *(toggle)*
- When enabled, viewed articles are recorded in the History screen
- When disabled, no new history is saved and the History screen remains empty

**Save Search History** *(toggle)*
- When enabled, search queries are saved for later reference
- When disabled, searches are not recorded

---

#### Default App

- An informational card explains how to set Wiki Reader as the default app to handle Wikipedia links (i.e., links opened from other apps that point to Wikipedia)
- Two buttons: **Settings** (opens device settings to configure default apps) and **Instructions** (provides step-by-step guidance)

---

### 6. About Screen

Provides information about the app, its dependencies, and its credits.

#### App Header
- App name, icon, and version number

#### App Information
- **Version** — displays the current app version
- **Source Code** — tappable link to the app's source code repository
- **Original App** — tappable link to the original Wikipedia reader app that inspired this one
- **License** — displays the license type (MIT) and links to the full license text

#### Built With
- Links to the major frameworks or tools used to build the app (no version-specific detail needed in the UI; just names with links to documentation)

#### Credits
- **Original Author** — name and GitHub profile link for the original app author
- **Donate** — link to support the original author (e.g., GitHub Sponsors)

#### Wikipedia
- **Wikipedia** — link to Wikipedia's main page
- **Support Wikipedia** — link to the Wikimedia Foundation donation page

All items on this screen are tappable and open their respective URLs in the device's default browser.

---

## Data & Storage

### Article Saving
- Users can save any Wikipedia article from the article view for later access
- Saved articles persist across app sessions
- Saved articles retain their title, description, URL, and language

### History
- Every Wikipedia article the user views is automatically recorded with a timestamp
- History entries include: title, description, URL, language, and date/time viewed
- History respects the "Save History" toggle in Settings

### Search History
- Search queries are stored if "Save Search History" is enabled
- Search history is accessible from the search interface

### Settings Persistence
- All user settings are persisted locally across sessions
- Default values apply on first launch

---

## Wikipedia Integration

- The app loads Wikipedia articles using standard Wikipedia URLs
- Supports all Wikipedia language editions
- Random articles are loaded via Wikipedia's built-in random article endpoint
- Articles render with their standard Wikipedia styling, subject to font and theme overrides applied by the app
- Internal Wikipedia links (clicking a link within an article) navigate within the app

---

## Deep Linking

- The app registers a custom URL scheme (`wikireader://`) for deep linking
- The app can be set as the default handler for Wikipedia URLs on the device, so that Wikipedia links opened from other apps open in Wiki Reader instead of a browser

---

## Display & Theming

- The app supports **light**, **dark**, and **system-default** themes
- An optional **black theme** variant uses pure black backgrounds in dark mode
- The accent/primary color is user-configurable from 13 preset options
- Font size and font family (serif vs. sans-serif) are user-configurable and apply to article text
- The theme and font settings propagate into the Wikipedia web view (articles reflect the app's dark/light mode and font preferences)

---

## Accessibility & UX

- The app is responsive to different screen sizes
- Immersive mode improves the reading experience on smaller screens
- Empty states on Saved and History screens include an icon and descriptive text to orient new users
- Loading states are communicated via a progress bar rather than a spinner, giving a sense of progress

---

## Out of Scope (Placeholder Features)

The following features are present in the UI as visual placeholders but are not functional in the current version:

- Article translation
- Article sharing
- Offline article download
- Full search functionality (beyond UI affordance)

These may be implemented in a future version.
